FILE FORMAT

Three columns is enough

One row per prediction: when it was made, what your model said, and what actually happened over the horizon. Any scale works for the prediction; the outcome is typically a forward return in percent.

timestamp,prediction,outcome,asset,price
2026-03-01 00:00,62.4,0.31,BTC,84210.5

Optional: a group column (asset, sector, segment) enables the per-group breakdown. A price or other level column enables the regime test. Use ISO dates or Unix time — ambiguous formats like 03/04/2026 can’t be read safely.

WHAT IT WON’T DO

No score out of 100

A single number gets quoted as “my model scored 82”. You get pass or fail per test, with the numbers behind it and what they mean. There is no parameter search and no “best threshold” — that is exactly where overfitting starts.

It doesn’t simulate a strategy, and it knows nothing about trading: sales forecasts, demand models, churn scores — anything you predict and later observe can be tested.

03

Six traps we fell into first

Every test exists because it caught a mistake we made ourselves, with real numbers. None of these look wrong until you check.

r = −0.39

Regime artefact

Our v1 engine’s 24-hour signal looked like a strong contrarian edge. Split into up-days and down-days, it was a short bias in a falling market.

Caught by: regime split
0.49 → 0.06

Window noise

A model score that collapsed when the validation window grew from 192 to 330 rows. The number was the window, not the model.

Caught by: effective sample size
3 of 8

Selection

We locked a claim that three coins carried the signal. The three held — but the “noise” coins turned positive too. The selection was the illusion.

Caught by: stability & per-group
+0.04% < 0.06%

Cost blindness

A real, monotone gap per trade — smaller than the cost of one round trip. Real signal, negative trade.

Caught by: net of costs
−53% → −29%

Mechanical risk reduction

An index that cut BTC’s worst drawdown almost in half. Random strategies with the same time in the market averaged −32%. Most of the effect was just being out of the market.

Caught by: permutation
145,800 ≠ 53,987

Duplicated rows

A data extract matched each score several times. The sample looked almost three times larger, and weak effects suddenly looked significant.

Caught by: duplicate check

CASE STUDY · WHY EVERY TEST SPLITS BY REGIME

The number that looked best was the most misleading

For a period, our v1 engine’s 24-hour results looked outstanding: a correlation of −0.39, with the lowest-conviction bucket finishing positive 72% of the time. Read at face value, that is a powerful contrarian signal, and it would have been easy to build a product on it.

It wasn’t a signal. It was a short bias that happened to coincide with a falling market. Split into up-days and down-days, the gap was positive on down-days and negative on up-days. A signal that only works while the market falls isn’t an edge — it’s a directional bet in disguise, and it breaks the moment conditions change.

What if my signal fails?

Then the report says so, and you pay the same. Most signals fail — ours did. A clear “no” with the reasons is usually the most useful thing you can get before you put money or reputation behind a claim.

Who sees my data?

Quick Check: nobody. The file is analysed in memory and deleted when the request ends. Validation Report: only us, for your case. It is deleted 30 days after delivery and never shared or reused.

Does it have to be crypto or trading?

No. The tests need three things: when a prediction was made, what it said, and what actually happened. Sales forecasts, demand models, churn scores and sports models work the same way.

My data is in Excel. Can I use it?

Yes — save it as CSV. Danish and European formats with semicolons and decimal commas are read automatically. The data guide shows the details.

What exactly gets tested in a report?

Your claim, in one sentence — for example “our score predicts 4-hour returns across eight coins after 0.06% costs”. It is locked with a timestamp and a hash when you submit it, so it can’t drift while we work.

Do you sell signals or give trading advice?

No. We don’t sell signals, manage money, trade, or take commissions from anyone we test. We judge statistics, not whether anyone should act on them.
