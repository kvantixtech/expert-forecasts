# Changelog

`METHOD.md` was committed before any result was computed (commit `ba72961`). Everything below was decided while collecting the data, before the scoring script was run for the first time. None of it changes a rule in `METHOD.md`; it records how the rules were applied where the method did not say.

## 2026-09-29: how the rules were applied

1. **Restated values count.** Two editions could not be retrieved in the original: ØR December 2015 and Danmarks Nationalbank December 2017. For these, the values come from the forecaster's own later publication, which prints what it forecast at the time: the ministry's history table (*Skøn på forskellige tidspunkter*, Tabel B.29 in ØR December 2016) and the Nationalbank's revision table (*Prognose fra december 2017*, Tabel A2 in its March 2018 outlook). Where both an original and such a restatement exist (NB December 2016 and September 2018), they agree. They are marked `restated` in `data/forecasts.csv` and included in the main tables. The method's `secondary` label is for press releases and news reports, and no value in the main tables comes from one of those.
2. **De Økonomiske Råd forecasts the private consumption deflator, not CPI or HICP.** Every autumn report from 2015 to 2024 says so in the table note: *"Forbrugerpriserne er udtrykt ved deflatoren for det private forbrug"*. Following "each forecaster is scored on the index it forecasts", DØR's inflation is scored against the private consumption deflator from Statistics Denmark (NAN1, P.31, current prices divided by chained 2020 prices).
3. **Computed outcomes keep two decimals.** The HICP annual average (from the monthly PRIS07 index) and the deflator growth are computed, not published, so they are kept to two decimals. Rounding them to one decimal would add noise that depends on the index base year. GDP and CPI are used as Statistics Denmark publishes them, to one decimal.
4. **Direction when the forecast says "no change".** If the forecast for Y+1 equals the forecaster's estimate for Y, it did not say whether the variable would rise or fall, so the year counts as not correct.
5. **First estimate = the first full release.** For GDP, the "first estimate" is the annual growth stated in Statistics Denmark's first full national accounts release for the fourth quarter (end of February), not the earlier flash indicator. The releases and quotes are in `data/first_estimates.csv`.
6. **Editions used where there was no December edition.** In 2022, ØR was published in August and not in December, because of the general election on 1 November 2022. The Nationalbank published in March and September in 2018, 2019 and from 2021, with an extra December edition in 2020. The "latest on or before 31 December" rule picks these editions. The notes in `data/forecasts.csv` give the evidence for each.
